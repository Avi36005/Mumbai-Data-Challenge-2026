A forecast is only useful if you know how far to trust it. AirPulse shows the 11–12 November PM2.5 outlook for four official stations, together with what the model could actually see, how often it has been right, and where the data itself is weak. It also tells you the cleanest hours to be outdoors.

What the model actually knows

The newest readings before this forecast were taken on fireworks nights, with hours above 500 µg/m³. Fed in raw, they would be mistaken for the new normal. So festival readings are kept, but capped at 1.25× each station’s usual level. Here is exactly what the model saw, and what it did not.

05

What the weather says

Pollution builds when air is trapped: weak wind and a shallow mixing layer. Ventilation (wind × mixing height) is the single best summary — low ventilation nights are smoggy nights. Rain washes the air.

06

Data health

A forecast inherits every flaw of its sensors. Monthly coverage of each station since February 2025, plus gaps, stuck readings and impossible values.

07

How it works — and what didn’t

The model

Two gradient-boosted tree models (LightGBM) averaged: one anchored at the newest usable PM reading, one trained for the exact forecast lead with NOAA GFS weather as it was issued at the time. 28-window backtests, no peeking at the future. Every submitted file can be rebuilt byte-for-byte from the code.

Two forecasts, not one

The competition scores the better of two files, so we submitted two honest scenarios: the expected level, and the same forecast with nights lifted by the pattern seen after Diwali 2019 and 2024 (about 1.6× in the small hours of 11 Nov, 1.0× by day). On past Diwalis this pair was never worse than a flat ×1.15 lift and 7–10% better in dry years.

Honest negatives

Ideas that sounded good but lost on blind tests: a second weather run (−6.9% on 5–6 Nov), stretching the daily cycle (wins only 25% of windows), feeding in the raw post-fireworks readings (three times worse in a rain year), starting the forecast nine hours later (no gain), and a much higher second file (passed its test, failed a sanity check against every past Diwali). We keep what survives.

Data
